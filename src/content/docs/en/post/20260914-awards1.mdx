## <M code="e65f"/> On the selection process
---
Thank you very much for participating in the "First RPG-Cobo Contest" and for submitting such wonderful works!

The new RPG-Cobo tool was announced on June 5, the contest announcement and start were on July 9, and the deadline was September 8, so it was a very tight schedule.  
It was a difficult decision for us as organizers whether planning the contest around the Tokyo Game Show exhibition was really the right choice.

Even so, many excellent works were submitted. **Being able to hold this contest during the beta test period was a very meaningful opportunity for RPG-Cobo.**

Unfortunately, there was no work selected for the Grand Prize, but there were many that came close, so we increased the number of Special Awards from 2 to 3.

## <M code="e0f0"/> Judges' comments on the selected works
---

#### Excellence Award: Florist of the Flower Magic Garden ~Floral Gardener~ (by Suzuran)

This was the largest and most polished entry among all submissions.
The battle difficulty rises at a good pace toward the later half, each minor enemy has its own personality, and brute force alone does not work against the bosses.  
The charming four player characters were also very well balanced in terms of abilities, equipment, and skills, making the game highly strategic for the player.

I think the overall polish could be improved even further by tightening the early game and making the quest flow easier to follow.

#### Excellence Award: Ruins of the Curse (by Nekko)

This game cleverly expresses a roguelike-style battle system using RPG-Cobo's basic event system, and the high-strategy gameplay was enjoyable.
I was especially impressed that different equipment combinations could choose styles such as evasion, counterattacks, and combo attacks, and even the skills that activate would change.  
The dance scene on the title screen and the opening presentation were also very carefully made, drawing the player into the world.

Although this was only a demo up to the first boss, it is a work I would very much like to see continued.

#### Special Award: Goddess of Truth (by Fukurou)

This work had story voice acting recorded throughout, which made the characters even more appealing.
The mysteries, the mechanisms and direction behind them, and all of the multiple endings were tightly connected, creating a powerfully unique world.

It was already full of surprises, but even more surprising was how skillfully AI-based systems and event construction were incorporated (I didn't notice until partway through...).
This is a great example showing how open-source RPG-Cobo is very easy to modify with AI.

#### Special Award: Find Quest (by Runi)

Compared with the other selected works, this one was created in a simpler style, but that simplicity made the fun of the game stand out even more.
The use of existing assets such as sample maps was very skillful, and there were many touches that made the maps and characters feel charming.
It also prepared both a normal ending and a true ending, making the outcome change based on the player's puzzle solving very appealing.

It was a fine piece that felt like it could be an ideal sample game for RPG-Cobo.

#### Special Award: Iris Code (by Mora Aosaki)

This is a wonderful work, but it was a shame it was so short; under normal selection criteria it would have remained an honorable mention.
However, the worldbuilding and especially the character illustrations were too charming to leave at that, and they were strong enough to make us want the creator to continue development and finish Iris Code.
That is why we increased the number of award slots and made it a Special Award.

Both the dream world and the real world make excellent use of RPG-Cobo's expressiveness, creating a very fantastical and beautiful world.

##### Judges' comments for honorable mentions

**Shiren-kun's Little Adventure Tale**: A one-quest short story, but the battle balance and event scene direction were very polished, and it felt like it could grow into a large-scale title if continued.

**Aisha's Debt Repayment Plan**: It had excellent minigame-level fun. The illustration of Aisha was always shown in the lower left, and it was enjoyable just to watch her facial expressions change.
I hope it gets a sequel with even higher difficulty.

**Aim for the Sacred UNK●**: A work characterized by free party composition and random dungeon elements.
The direction and ease of play were also carefully thought out, making it a very strong game system.

**Meskaki Asure (tentative)**: A pure athletic game. It makes very effective use of RPG-Cobo's "moving terrain" system and is both fun and high-difficulty.
I couldn't clear it without mistakes because it was too difficult, so I cheated by laying a ladder to the goal and cleared it, but there was another stage after that! (That one was still in development.)  
It strongly suggested the potential for making athletic games in RPG-Cobo.

**Neko-chan Quest**: A work that collected multiple minigames, enthusiastically using many RPG-Cobo features. It also had playful touches throughout, including a bonus stage.  
I think it would become even more fun if the difficulty were raised further to make it more challenging.

**RE:FRAME DOLL**: It makes excellent use of AI, completely rebuilds the game UI, creates characters by swapping parts, and allows battle factions to be specified,
greatly extending RPG-Cobo's core systems. It was a very exciting work that makes us want to see how far it will grow in the future.


## Closing remarks

In this contest, due in part to time constraints, there were not many long RPGs, and many works ended early, so compact works tended to be rated relatively higher.
Even for the works that were not selected this time, many gave the impression that they would have reached sufficient completion if there had been a little more time.

Reflecting on this, we want to improve the contest rules and schedule from next time so that any type of work can be submitted with enough time.

Another point worth noting is that there were several works that used AI. While AI use expanded the potential for creating amazing works, we also saw bugs and drawbacks that would not normally occur,
and we felt the difficulty and challenge of using AI as well. We would like to consider whether the tool side can provide more support in this area.

Finally, the lessons learned through the feedback from this contest are immeasurable and will be extremely valuable for future RPG-Cobo development.  
We promise that by the next contest, RPG-Cobo will have evolved further and be even closer to the best RPG creation tool.

We look forward to your participation in the next contest.
